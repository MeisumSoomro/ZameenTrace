Why ZameenTrace Exists

The system that was supposed to protect you doesn't.

Pakistan holds approximately 48 million agricultural parcels — most undocumented with any meaningful precision. Boundaries exist in collective memory. Records live in paper registers scattered across thousands of patwari offices.

When a farmer needs to sell, lease, or use land as loan collateral, they enter a system designed around information asymmetry. Intermediaries thrive precisely because buyers and sellers cannot verify anything independently.

Satellite images exist but aren't interpreted. Documents exist but can't be authenticated quickly. The result: even sophisticated investors fly to a site before spending a rupee — because no digital truth exists.

Intelligence Engine

How the Intelligence Engine works

Four stages. Each one adding a layer of certainty. Click any stage to see what happens inside.

01
Discover
Locate and identify the parcel
Coordinate match

Cross-reference coordinates against provincial revenue databases, NDMA satellite grids, and historical patwari records to establish baseline parcel identity.

Boundary extraction

Existing boundary markers extracted from satellite imagery using edge-detection models trained on Pakistani agricultural land typology.

Owner history pull

Where digitized records exist, we surface the chain of ownership going back to the earliest available document — currently up to 40 years in pilot districts.

Adjacent parcel scan

All parcels within 50 metres are identified and flagged, enabling proactive dispute detection before verification begins.

02
Verify
Ground-truth with human confirmation
GPS field capture

Boundary coordinates captured at each corner with sub-metre precision using RTK-enabled devices. Timestamps and GPS metadata cryptographically sealed on capture.

Neighbour consensus

Adjacent landowners invited to confirm or dispute via the mobile app. Disputes trigger human review before the record is finalized.

Document validation

Fard, Inteqal, and sale deed documents checked against known formatting patterns and cross-matched with digitized revenue records.

Confidence scoring

Each verification receives a score (0–100) based on satellite match, neighbour confirmation, document validity, and field capture quality.

03
Analyse
Derive intelligence from verified data
Satellite analysis

Multi-temporal imagery reveals land-use change, crop rotation patterns, flood risk, and encroachment indicators over a configurable time window.

Infrastructure mapping

Road access quality, irrigation proximity, electricity coverage, and market distance computed from open geospatial datasets and proprietary infrastructure layers.

Price benchmarking

Transaction comparables from verified neighbouring parcels generate a fair-value range, reducing information asymmetry between buyers and sellers.

Risk modelling

Flood plains, seismic zones, contested boundary histories, and encroachment patterns combined into a composite risk score with plain-language explanations.

04
Report
Deliver structured land intelligence
Structured report

A Land Intelligence Report generated covering ownership, verification status, satellite analysis, risk assessment, infrastructure scoring, and area growth metrics.

Shareable record

Reports cryptographically signed and shareable via a permanent, tamper-evident URL — no login required to verify authenticity.

API delivery

For institutional clients, structured JSON responses available via the ZameenTrace Intelligence API for integration into loan origination and investment platforms.

Audit trail

Every data point includes a source citation, a timestamp, and a confidence indicator — so downstream users know exactly how reliable each element is.

Building Trust Through Intelligence

Pakistan's land deserves a record as permanent as the ground itself.

For generations, the value of land in Pakistan has been held hostage to fragmented records, informal knowledge, and intermediaries who profit from information asymmetry. A farmer in Sindh shouldn't need six months and a lawyer's fee to prove ownership of land their family has farmed for forty years.

ZameenTrace is built on the belief that trust is a technical problem — that with enough verified data, enough satellite coverage, and enough ground-level confirmation, the ambiguity that enables exploitation can be systematically removed.

This is not a startup play on Pakistani real estate. It is a long project aimed at creating the infrastructure that makes the market function honestly — for farmers, investors, lenders, and eventually for the state itself.

We are early. We are building in Sindh first. We would rather do one district well than ten districts poorly.
