Why ZameenTrace Exists

The system that was supposed to protect you doesn't.

Pakistan holds approximately 48 million agricultural parcels — most of them undocumented with any meaningful precision. Boundaries exist in collective memory. Records live in paper registers across thousands of patwari offices.

When a farmer needs to sell, lease, or use land as loan collateral, they enter a system designed around information asymmetry. Intermediaries thrive precisely because buyers and sellers cannot verify anything independently.

Satellite images exist but aren't interpreted. Documents exist but can't be authenticated quickly. The result: even sophisticated investors fly to a site before spending a rupee — because no digital truth exists.

How the Intelligence Engine Works

Every verification request flows through four stages. Click each to explore what happens inside.

01 Discover Locate and identify the parcel
Coordinate match

We cross-reference entered coordinates against provincial revenue databases, NDMA satellite grids, and historical patwari records to establish baseline parcel identity.

Boundary extraction

Existing boundary markers are extracted from satellite imagery using edge-detection models trained on Pakistani agricultural land typology.

Owner history pull

Where digitized records exist, we surface the chain of ownership going back to the earliest available document — currently up to 40 years in pilot districts.

Adjacent parcel scan

All parcels within 50 metres are identified and flagged, enabling proactive dispute detection before verification begins.

02 Verify Ground-truth with human confirmation
GPS field capture

Field agents capture boundary coordinates at each corner with sub-metre precision using RTK-enabled devices. Timestamps and GPS metadata are cryptographically sealed on capture.

Neighbor consensus

Adjacent landowners are contacted and invited to confirm or dispute the captured boundary via the farmer-facing mobile app. Disputes trigger human review before finalization.

Document validation

Uploaded Fard, Inteqal, and sale deed documents are checked against known formatting patterns and cross-matched with digitized revenue records.

Confidence scoring

Each verification receives a confidence score (0–100) based on the number of corroborating signals: satellite match, neighbor confirmation, document validity, and field capture quality.

03 Analyse Derive intelligence from verified data
Satellite analysis

Multi-temporal satellite imagery reveals land-use change, crop rotation patterns, flood risk, and encroachment indicators over a configurable time window.

Infrastructure mapping

Road access quality, irrigation proximity, electricity coverage, and market distance are computed from open geospatial datasets and proprietary infrastructure layers.

Price benchmarking

Transaction comparables from verified neighboring parcels generate a fair-value range, reducing information asymmetry between buyers and sellers.

Risk modelling

Flood plains, seismic zones, contested boundary histories, and encroachment patterns are combined into a composite risk score with plain-language explanations.

04 Report Deliver structured land intelligence
Structured report

A Land Intelligence Report is generated covering ownership, verification status, satellite analysis, risk assessment, infrastructure scoring, and area growth metrics — all in one document.

Shareable record

Reports are cryptographically signed and can be shared with banks, buyers, or legal counsel via a permanent, tamper-evident URL that doesn't require any login to verify.

API delivery

For institutional clients, structured JSON responses are available via the ZameenTrace Intelligence API, enabling integration into loan origination systems and investment platforms.

Audit trail

Every data point in the report includes a source citation, a timestamp, and a confidence indicator — so downstream users know exactly how reliable each element is.

Building Trust Through Intelligence

Pakistan's land deserves a record as permanent as the ground itself.

For generations, the value of land in Pakistan has been held hostage to fragmented records, informal knowledge, and intermediaries who profit from information asymmetry. A farmer in Sindh shouldn't need to spend six months and a lawyer's fee to prove they own what their family has farmed for forty years.

ZameenTrace is built on the belief that trust is a technical problem — that with enough verified data, enough satellite coverage, and enough ground-level confirmation, the ambiguity that enables exploitation can be systematically removed.

This is not a startup play on Pakistani real estate. It is a long project aimed at creating the infrastructure that makes the market function honestly — for farmers, for investors, for lenders, and eventually for the state.

We are early. We are building in Sindh first. We would rather do one district well than ten districts poorly.
